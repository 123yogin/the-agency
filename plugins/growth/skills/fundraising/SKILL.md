# Fundraising

Investor materials that are consistent, credible and easy to defend in a
partner meeting, and outreach that is short and specific.

## Hard rules

- **One source of truth first.** Before drafting anything, write
  `fundraise/facts.md`: traction metrics (with dates), pricing and revenue
  assumptions, raise size and instrument, use of funds, team titles,
  milestones. If two numbers conflict anywhere, stop and resolve them.
- **Every number traces to that file.** Cross-check before delivering.
- **No unverifiable claims, no invented traction, logos or quotes.**
- **Assumptions are visible**, never buried in a formula.
- **Market sizing shows its math** (bottom-up preferred).
- **Not legal or securities advice.** Raising money is regulated (e.g. US
  Regulation D limits on general solicitation; similar rules elsewhere). Have
  counsel review instruments (SAFE, convertible note, priced round), terms and
  any public announcement of the raise.

## Workflow

1. Inventory canonical facts → `facts.md`.
2. List missing assumptions and ask for them.
3. Choose the asset.
4. Draft with explicit logic.
5. Quality gate (below).

## Pitch deck (10–12 slides + appendix)

1. Company + wedge (one sentence: what you do, for whom)
2. Problem (specific, evidenced)
3. Solution
4. Product / demo (real screenshots)
5. Why now
6. Market (bottom-up sizing)
7. Business model (pricing, unit economics if known)
8. Traction (dated, real; growth rate and retention beat totals)
9. Competition and differentiation (honest; include "do nothing")
10. Team (why this team wins)
11. The ask: amount, instrument, use of funds tied to milestones
12. Appendix: model summary, metrics definitions, details

## Market sizing

- **Bottom-up:** number of reachable customers in the beachhead × realistic
  annual price = serviceable market. Then show the expansion path.
- **Top-down** only as a sanity check, with the source and year.
- TAM / SAM / SOM each with assumptions written out. Never cite one big analyst
  number as your market.

## One-pager / memo

What the company does (one sentence) → why now → traction and proof early →
market → model → team → precise ask. Every claim easy to verify.

## Financial model

Explicit assumptions tab · revenue built layer by layer (customers × price ×
retention) · costs tied to hiring plan and milestones · bear / base / bull
cases where it matters · sensitivity on the 2–3 assumptions the outcome hinges
on · runway and the milestone the raise buys. Totals must sum; check with `python3`.

## Accelerator applications

Answer the exact question asked, in the word limit. Lead with traction,
insight and why this team. No puffery. Same numbers as everywhere else.

## Investor outreach

**Cold email**
1. Subject: short and specific (`<Company>: <wedge> — <one traction fact>`)
2. Opener: why this investor specifically (portfolio fit, a thesis post, a mutual contact)
3. Pitch: what you do, why now, the one proof point that matters
4. Ask: one concrete step ("15 minutes next week?" / "May I send the deck?")
5. Sign-off: name, role, one credibility anchor
Under ~150 words. If you can't personalise it, say the draft isn't ready.

**Bans:** "I'd love to connect", "excited to share", generic thesis praise,
vague founder adjectives, begging, soft questions when a direct ask is clearer.

**Follow-ups:** day 4–5 with one new data point; day 10–12 a clean final note.
Stop there unless the user wants more.

**Warm intro request:** why it's a fit for the investor (not just you) + a
forwardable blurb under 100 words + permission to say no.

**After a meeting:** what was discussed, the answer you promised, one new proof
point, the next step.

**Investor update (monthly/quarterly):** headline metrics vs last period ·
wins · lowlights (honest) · asks (specific intros, hires) · runway.

## Quality gate

- [ ] Every number matches `facts.md`
- [ ] Use of funds and revenue layers sum correctly
- [ ] Assumptions visible; market math shown
- [ ] No hype words; claims verifiable
- [ ] Each outreach email personalised to the investor
- [ ] Counsel flagged for instrument/terms/public statements
